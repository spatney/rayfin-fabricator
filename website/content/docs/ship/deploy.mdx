---
title: "Publish to Microsoft Fabric"
description: "Publish your app to a Fabric workspace, choose whether chat turns publish it automatically, and manage the workspaces it's published to."
---

Publishing puts the app in your current project into a Fabric workspace, where the people you share it with can use it. Fabricator runs the project's own `rayfin up`, records the published app that Rayfin creates, and shows it in the preview. Earlier versions of Fabricator called this deploying.

Rayfin apps are published as Fabric apps in a Fabric workspace. For Rayfin's platform details, see [Deploy Fabric apps](https://rayfin.ai/docs/deploy/fabric-apps). Publishing uses your Fabric capacity; see [Rayfin pricing and capacity notes](https://rayfin.ai/docs/deploy/pricing).

How publishing fits in depends on the **Local development** experiment:

- **Off**, the default: you develop each app against its published copy. A new app is published before you chat about it, and Fabricator publishes your changes after each chat turn.
- **On**: your app runs on this computer, and you publish it when you choose. See [Develop on this computer](/docs/build/local-development).

## Sign in to Fabric

Fabricator publishes with the Rayfin CLI's Fabric sign-in. You can sign in from the account control in the top-right app bar:

1. Select **Sign in to Fabric**.
2. Finish the browser sign-in that Rayfin opens.
3. Return to Fabricator and publish again if needed.

The workspace picker also prompts you when Fabricator needs Fabric credentials to list workspaces. If credentials expire, it may show "Sign in to Microsoft Fabric to list your workspaces."

You can stay signed in to more than one Fabric account, for example one per organization, and choose which one publishes. The **Published apps** panel names the account that publishes under **Account**, such as "alice@contoso.com · Contoso". Select **Change** to switch accounts. See [Manage your accounts](/docs/start/accounts).

## Publish for the first time

With Local development off, new projects ask to be published right after you name them. In **Publish your app**, pick a workspace and select **Publish app**. Chat stays locked until the app is published. See [Build your first app](/docs/start/first-app#publish-your-app).

![The Publish your app step of a new project, with Development as the name, a list of Fabric workspaces with their region and capacity, the Publish app button, and Continue without publishing.](/screenshots/first-deploy.webp)

**Continue without publishing →** skips the step: "You can publish any time, but chat stays locked until your app is published." To come back to it, select **Publish now** in the chat.

With Local development on, a new app has no publish step: it runs on this computer from the start. A project you opened from a folder or cloned has none either. Publish these from the app bar:

1. Open the project.
2. In the app bar, select **Publish**.
3. In **Publish to a Fabric workspace**, enter a **Name** if you want one, such as `Production`.
4. Under **Publish to workspace**, choose a Fabric workspace.
5. Select **Publish**.

Above the form, "Publishes as" names the Fabric account that publishes. Select **Change** to use another.

Eligible workspaces are on a Fabric **F-SKU** capacity, a Power BI Premium **P-SKU** capacity, or a dedicated capacity whose SKU Fabricator cannot see. Workspaces without an eligible capacity are listed under **Not eligible** with the reason. If Fabricator cannot find an eligible workspace, it offers **Start a free trial** and **Buy a capacity** links.

When your apps already use some eligible workspaces, the list starts with up to five of them under **Recent**, the workspace of the app you published or opened most recently first. The rest follow under **Other workspaces**.

To create a workspace from Fabricator, select **+ New workspace**, choose an active F-SKU or P-SKU capacity, enter a name, and select **Create workspace**. The new workspace uses the selected capacity's region.

## Same-name warning

Before a first publish into a workspace, Fabricator checks whether that workspace already has a Rayfin app with the same item name from `rayfin/rayfin.yml`.

If it finds one, you see a warning that publishing there would replace that app, for example "Operations already has an app named “Contoso Expenses”. Publishing here would replace it, so pick another workspace." Choose another workspace, or select **Replace it anyway** to confirm.

## What happens while your app publishes

When the preview has no app to show yet, such as during an app's first publish with Local development off, it shows the publish screen. Ray talks you through it, and below him a list shows the steps publishing has been through, the one in progress, and the steps still to come.

Otherwise, the app stays in the preview while it publishes, so you can keep looking at it: your published app, or with Local development on, your app on this computer. A strip above the preview shows where it's publishing to, such as "Publishing to Production", the step in progress, a progress bar and how long it has taken. Select **Watch** to follow it on the publish screen, and **Back to your app** to return. Select **Logs** to see the publish log. When the published app is in the preview, **Design** and the **Fabric** view wait until publishing ends, and the preview reloads when the new version is live.

The publish control in the app bar shows the progress too, so you can follow it from the Code, Blueprint and Advisor tabs.

With Rayfin CLI 1.35 or newer, the steps come from the CLI's own progress reports, and the step in progress shows the CLI's words for what it's doing. A typical publish goes through:

1. **Connecting to Fabric**
2. **Finding your workspace**
3. **Setting up your app in Fabric**
4. **Applying settings**
5. **Updating your database**, for apps that store data
6. **Building and uploading your app**
7. **Going live**

Some publishes have other steps, such as **Installing packages** when the project's packages are missing, **Getting Fabric capacity ready**, **Setting up file storage**, **Connecting your data sources** or **Publishing your functions**. If the CLI reports a step Fabricator doesn't know, the list shows it in the CLI's own words.

Projects on Rayfin CLI 1.34 or older show **Connecting to Fabric**, **Preparing to publish**, **Building your app**, **Packaging assets**, **Uploading to Fabric** and **Going live** instead. To get the detailed steps, update the project's Rayfin. See [Keep Rayfin up to date](/docs/ship/rayfin-versions).

If Fabric asks you to sign in again, publishing waits for you to finish in your browser. If you've [sent Ray away](/docs/start/tour#ray), the Fabricator logo takes his place.

![The publish screen: Ray says "Building your app. Fins crossed!" above Publishing Contoso Expenses and a list of steps. Five are checked off, Building and uploading your app is in progress with the CLI's words "Deploying static content" under it, and Going live is still to come.](/screenshots/deploy-progress.webp)

During publishing you can select **View logs** on the publish screen. If publishing fails, Ray stays above the preview and says "Your app didn’t make it to Fabric. Want me to find out why?" He offers **Find out why**, which opens Help to explain what went wrong, and **View logs** for the publish log. When Fabric wants you to sign in again, he offers **Refresh Fabric authentication** too. See [A deploy failed](/docs/troubleshooting/deploy#a-deploy-failed).

## Publish changes

After the first publish, the publish control in the app bar names the published app, with its state as a dot: green while it's live, red after a publish that didn't finish. Before that, it says **Not published**. The button beside it says what publishing would do now:

| Button | When it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Publish** | Before the first publish, the button is highlighted and opens the publish form. Once everything is published, it's quiet, and selecting it runs `rayfin up` again for the active published app. |
| **Publish 3 changes** | Files changed since the app was last published; the number counts them. When Fabricator can't tell which version is published, it says **Publish changes**. |
| **Try again** | The last publish didn't finish, and the publish control says **· Failed** after the name. Open it to see what went wrong, and select **Find out why** to ask Help. |
| **Publishing…** | Publishing is running, with how far it has got, such as **Publishing… 40%**. Select it to see the step in progress. |

While Copilot is working on a turn, the button is turned off: publishing then would send a half-finished change. Its tooltip says "Copilot is working on this app. Publishing waits until the turn finishes."

## Publish after each chat turn

**Publish after each chat turn** decides whether Fabricator publishes your changes when a successful chat turn ends. Until you change it, it's on with Local development off, and off with Local development on. Your choice applies to all your apps, and Fabricator remembers it.

1. In the app bar, select the publish control, which shows the published app's name.
2. Turn **Publish after each chat turn** on or off.

When your choice isn't the default, the publish control says so after the name: **· Manual** while it's off with Local development off, for example **Prod · Manual**, and **· Auto** while it's on with Local development on. You can also use [Search](/docs/start/search): **Turn on Publish after each chat turn** or **Turn off Publish after each chat turn**.

### While it's on

Fabricator publishes after a successful chat turn when the app has changes that aren't published. This includes edits that Copilot already committed to the project's git history. It doesn't publish when:

- the chat turn failed or was cancelled,
- there are no changes since the last published version,
- a team workspace pipeline publishes the app,
- with Local development on, the app hasn't been published yet: an app's first publish is yours to start,
- Fabricator can't check for changes. It says "Could not check for changes to publish. Select Publish to publish your changes." Select **Publish** to publish them yourself.

With Local development off, a turn also publishes an app that has never been published, such as a project you opened from a folder. The preview then asks you to **Choose where to publish**: select **Publish** in the app bar to name the published app and pick its workspace. See [The preview says Choose where to publish](/docs/troubleshooting/deploy#the-preview-says-choose-where-to-publish).

While an after-turn publish runs, the preview keeps showing what it showed during the turn. With Local development off, that's the local preview with your changes, when the turn had one, and once publishing ends, the preview shows your published app. With Local development on, your app on this computer stays in the preview.

When Fabricator switches to another published app or can't tell which version is published, the next successful turn publishes once to set a new baseline.

### While it's off

Your changes stay on this computer until you publish them. The button in the app bar counts them, for example **Publish 3 changes**: select it when you're ready. Your app stays in the preview while it publishes.

With Local development on, that's the only difference: your app on this computer shows your changes either way.

With Local development off, the published app falls behind your code, so the preview shows your app from a local Vite server whenever the app is open, at a `localhost` address, not only while Copilot is working:

- The local preview uses your published app's backend. Frontend changes show as Copilot makes them, but changes to data, permissions or functions work only after you publish them. Fabricator asks Copilot to tell you when a change needs a publish.
- When a turn that changed your app ends, the preview reloads once, so it shows the finished app from a fresh start, not only the live updates made while Copilot worked. A turn that changed nothing, such as a question, leaves the page as it is.
- **Design** works on the local preview between turns, so you can queue visual changes and send them to Copilot without publishing first. It turns off when a turn starts, because Copilot's changes show in the preview as it makes them. The **Fabric** view works on the published app, so it's unavailable.
- The local preview stops when you switch to another app or close Fabricator, and the app you open next gets its own. If you switch your app to another published app, its local preview starts again with that one's settings.
- If the project has no local Vite installation, there's no local preview. The preview keeps showing the published app, without your latest changes, until you publish them.
- Team apps aren't affected. They still save to GitHub after each turn, and the team pipeline deploys your preview.

Turning **Publish after each chat turn** back on closes the local preview and doesn't publish anything. Your changes are still on this computer: the next successful chat turn publishes them, or you can publish them from the app bar.

## Work with more than one published app

In the app bar, select the publish control, which shows the published app's name, to open the **Published apps** panel. It opens straight away and doesn't sign you in to Fabric. It shows:

- the published app's name and state, as a dot and words, for example "Published 2 h ago". Select the pencil, **Rename this published app**, to rename it.
- what's waiting to publish, with **See what changed**, which opens **History** in the Code tab,
- **App**, the app's address. Select **Copy link** to copy it, or **Open in your browser**.
- **Workspace**, the Fabric workspace, with its region and capacity. Select **Open the workspace in Fabric** to open it in the Fabric portal. The region and capacity show once you're signed in to Fabric.
- **Account**, the Fabric account that publishes. Select **Change** to use another, or **Sign in** when you're signed out.
- **Other published apps**, the project's other published apps. Select **Switch** on one to make it the active published app: the preview shows it, and publishing goes to it. Point at one to rename it.
- **Publish after each chat turn**. See [Publish after each chat turn](/docs/ship/deploy#publish-after-each-chat-turn).
- **Publish to another workspace…**, to publish the same project to another workspace.

**Share** is next to the publish control once the app is published. See [Share your app](/docs/ship/share).

Switching uses Rayfin's recorded deployments for the project. Each published app is in its own Fabric workspace.

Fabricator doesn't delete published apps from this panel.

## Refresh Fabric authentication

If publishing repeatedly fails even though you recently signed in, use **Refresh Fabric authentication** from the account menu. When publishing fails because Fabric wants you to sign in again, the notice above the preview offers it too. Fabricator runs the selected project's `rayfin logout`, then `rayfin login`, verifies the new Fabric credential, and doesn't publish your app again by itself. After it succeeds, publish again from the app bar.

See [setup and sign-in troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting/setup-and-sign-in) and [publishing troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting/deploy) for recovery steps.

## Redirect URIs for live preview

A local preview of a published app runs against the published backend: with Local development off, for team apps, and for a published app this computer can't run. Fabricator runs the project's own Vite dev server on an allowed `localhost` port. If the normal sign-in-compatible ports are busy and you choose another port, Fabricator can add that origin to `services.auth.allowedRedirectUris` in `rayfin/rayfin.yml` and push the runtime settings to Fabric without uploading the app. This keeps Fabric sign-in working in the live preview. The next publish includes the app code. See [When every preview port is busy](/docs/build/preview#when-every-preview-port-is-busy).
