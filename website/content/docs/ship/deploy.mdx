Deploying publishes the app in your current project to Microsoft Fabric. Fabricator runs the project's own `rayfin up`, records the deployment that Rayfin creates, and then loads the app in the preview.

Rayfin apps deploy as Fabric apps in a Fabric workspace. For Rayfin's platform details, see [Deploy Fabric apps](https://rayfin.ai/docs/deploy/fabric-apps). Deployment uses your Fabric capacity; see [Rayfin pricing and capacity notes](https://rayfin.ai/docs/deploy/pricing).

![The deployments panel listing the active Development deployment with its workspace, region, capacity and app address, and a New deployment link.](/screenshots/deployments.webp)

## Sign in to Fabric

Fabricator uses the Rayfin CLI credentials for Fabric deployment. You can sign in from the account control in the top-right app bar:

1. Select **Sign in to Fabric**.
2. Finish the browser sign-in that Rayfin opens.
3. Return to Fabricator and deploy again if needed.

The deployments picker also prompts you when Fabricator needs Fabric credentials to list workspaces. If credentials expire, it may show "Sign in to Microsoft Fabric to list your workspaces."

You can stay signed in to more than one Fabric account, for example one per organization, and choose which one deploys. The **Deployments** panel names the account that deploys, such as "Deploys as alice@contoso.com · Contoso". Select **Change** to switch accounts. See [Manage your accounts](/docs/start/accounts).

## Deploy for the first time

New projects ask for their first deployment right after you name them. In **Deploy your app**, pick a workspace and choose **Deploy app**; see [Build your first app](/docs/start/first-app#create-the-first-deployment). Chat stays locked until the app has its first Fabric deployment.

![The Deploy your app step of a new project, with a deployment name, a list of Fabric workspaces with their region and capacity, and the Deploy app button.](/screenshots/first-deploy.webp)

If you chose **Continue without deploying →**, or opened a project that has never been deployed, deploy it from the app bar:

1. Open the project.
2. In the app bar, select **Deploy**.
3. In **New deployment**, enter a friendly **Name** if you want one, such as `Production`.
4. Under **Deploy to workspace**, choose a Fabric workspace.
5. Select **Create & deploy**.

Eligible workspaces are on a Fabric **F-SKU** capacity, a Power BI Premium **P-SKU** capacity, or a dedicated capacity whose SKU Fabricator cannot see. Workspaces without an eligible capacity are listed under **Not eligible** with the reason. If Fabricator cannot find an eligible workspace, it offers **Start a free trial** and **Buy a capacity** links.

To create a workspace from Fabricator, select **+ New workspace**, choose an active F-SKU or P-SKU capacity, enter a name, and select **Create workspace**. The new workspace uses the selected capacity's region.

## Same-name warning

Before a first deploy into a workspace, Fabricator checks whether that workspace already has a Rayfin app with the same item name from `rayfin/rayfin.yml`.

If it finds one, you see a warning that deploying there would replace that app. Choose another workspace, or select **Replace it anyway** to confirm.

## What happens during deploy

While a deploy runs, the preview shows the deploy screen. Ray talks you through it, and below him a list shows the steps the deploy has been through, the one in progress, and the steps still to come.

With Rayfin CLI 1.35 or newer, the steps come from the CLI's own progress reports, and the step in progress shows the CLI's words for what it's doing. A typical deploy goes through:

1. **Connecting to Fabric**
2. **Finding your workspace**
3. **Setting up your app in Fabric**
4. **Applying settings**
5. **Updating your database**, for apps that store data
6. **Building and uploading your app**
7. **Going live**

Some deploys have other steps, such as **Installing packages** when the project's packages are missing, **Getting Fabric capacity ready**, **Setting up file storage**, **Connecting your data sources** or **Deploying your functions**. If the CLI reports a step Fabricator doesn't know, the list shows it in the CLI's own words.

Projects on Rayfin CLI 1.34 or older show **Connecting to Fabric**, **Preparing deployment**, **Building your app**, **Packaging assets**, **Uploading to Fabric** and **Going live** instead. To get the detailed steps, update the project's Rayfin. See [Keep Rayfin up to date](/docs/ship/rayfin-versions).

If Fabric asks you to sign in again, the deploy waits for you to finish in your browser. With Ray turned off in **Settings → Appearance**, the Fabricator logo takes his place.

![The deploy screen: Ray says "Building your app. Fins crossed!" above a list of steps. Five are checked off, Building and uploading your app is in progress with the CLI's words "Deploying static content" under it, and Going live is still to come.](/screenshots/deploy-progress.webp)

During the deploy you can select **View logs** in the deploy screen. If the deploy fails, Ray stays above the preview and offers **Find out why**, which opens Help to explain what went wrong, and **View logs** for the deploy's output. See [A deploy failed](/docs/troubleshooting/deploy#a-deploy-failed).

## Redeploy changes

After the first successful deployment, the primary button changes to **Redeploy**. Select it to run `rayfin up` again for the active deployment.

Fabricator also redeploys automatically after a successful Copilot turn when the project has undeployed changes. This includes edits that Copilot already committed to the project's git history. It does not redeploy when:

- the Copilot turn failed or was cancelled,
- the [Deploy manually](/docs/ship/deploy#deploy-manually) experiment is set to **Manual deploy**,
- there are no content changes since the last deployed revision,
- a team workspace pipeline owns the app's deployment,
- Fabricator cannot check whether changes are undeployed.

If change detection fails, Fabricator shows "Could not check for undeployed changes." followed by "Use Redeploy to publish your changes." Use **Redeploy** to publish explicitly.

When Fabricator switches to another deployment or cannot identify the deployed git baseline, the next successful turn deploys once to establish a new baseline.

## Deploy manually

> [!WARNING]
> Deploy manually is an experiment. Turn it on in **Settings → Experiments**. It may change or be removed in a future update.

Turn on **Deploy manually** to make several changes in chat before you deploy. Chat turns no longer deploy your app: your changes stay on this computer until you select **Redeploy**. Because the deployed app falls behind your code, the preview shows your app from a local Vite server whenever the app is open, not only while Copilot is working.

1. Open **Settings**, then expand **Experiments**.
2. Turn on **Deploy manually**.
3. Open your app. The status bar shows **Manual deploy**, and the preview shows your local app, marked **Local**.
4. Keep chatting. Changes show in the preview as Copilot makes them.
5. When you're ready, select **Redeploy** in the app bar. The preview shows the deploy's progress, then your local app again.

To go back to deploying after each turn for a while, choose **Auto deploy** in the status bar, and **Manual deploy** to switch back. The experiment stays on, so the switch stays in the status bar. Turning **Deploy manually** on again starts you in **Manual deploy**.

While the status bar shows **Manual deploy**:

- The local preview uses your deployed app's backend. Frontend changes show as Copilot makes them, but changes to data, permissions or functions work only after you select **Redeploy**. Fabricator asks Copilot to tell you when a change needs a deploy.
- **Design** works on the local preview, so you can queue visual changes and send them to Copilot without deploying. The **Fabric** view remains unavailable while the local preview is showing.
- The local preview stops when you switch to another app or close Fabricator, and the app you open next gets its own. If you switch your app to another deployment, its local preview starts again with that deployment's settings.
- If the project has no local Vite installation, there's no local preview. The preview keeps showing the deployed app, without your latest changes, until you select **Redeploy**.
- Team apps aren't affected. They still save to GitHub after each turn, and the team pipeline deploys your preview.

Switching to **Auto deploy**, or turning **Deploy manually** off, closes the local preview and doesn't deploy anything. Your changes are still on this computer: the next successful chat turn deploys them, or you can select **Redeploy**.

## Work with multiple deployments

In the app bar, select the deployment control, marked with a cloud icon, to open **Deployments**. From there you can:

- see the active deployment,
- see which Fabric account deploys, and select **Change** to use another,
- select **+ New deployment** to deploy the same project to another workspace,
- select **Switch** on another recorded deployment,
- rename a deployment by selecting its name,
- select **Refresh deployments** to reload the list,
- select **Share** after a deployment exists.

Switching uses Rayfin's recorded deployments for the project. Each deployment points to a Fabric workspace and may show its workspace region and capacity SKU.

Fabricator does not show a per-deployment delete action in this panel.

## Refresh Fabric authentication

If deployment repeatedly fails even though you recently signed in, use **Refresh Fabric authentication** from the account menu. When a deploy fails because Fabric wants you to sign in again, the notice above the preview offers it too. Fabricator runs the selected project's `rayfin logout`, then `rayfin login`, verifies the new Fabric credential, and does not redeploy automatically. After it succeeds, select **Redeploy**.

See [setup and sign-in troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting/setup-and-sign-in) and [deploy troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting/deploy) for recovery steps.

## Redirect URIs for live preview

When a chat turn starts, Fabricator may run the project's local Vite preview on an allowed `localhost` port. If the normal sign-in-compatible ports are busy and you choose another port, Fabricator can add that origin to `services.auth.allowedRedirectUris` in `rayfin/rayfin.yml` and push the runtime settings to Fabric without uploading the app. This keeps Fabric sign-in working in the live preview. The next normal deploy publishes the app code.
